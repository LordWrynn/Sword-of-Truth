|* A basic guide to creating a character *|
	When creating a character, try to imagine them as a person in your mind. This process is as much developing a character as it is writing down stats. Your character needs a couple important things, like a name, gender, age, and aesthetic details. Once you have an idea of your character, then comes the stats.
	
	The first stat you need to roll is your Han, which is the basis for your ability to use magic. Use a 1d10 to determine your Caster Tier:
				1-5 = No magical ability
				6-7 = Very low magic ability
				8   = Low magical ability
				9   = Moderate magical ability
				10  = Highly gifted
				
	If your Caster Tier is greater than 5, then roll 1d20 and multiply the result by half of your Caster Tier. This is your Han stat for the game.
		Example: You roll an 8 on your Caster Tier, and then a 13. Your Han stat would be 52. ( (8/2) * 13  = 52)
		
	Next, roll for your Base Stats. Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, and Vitality.

	Strength:
		1 to 100 scale. An average adult has 30 or so, a child around 5-10, soliders and physical laborers around 50, and First File soliders around 70. Beyond 70 are the superfreak "Andre the Giant" style people that are unnatural in one way or another.
			Determine this number with a 4d20+10 roll.
		
	Dexterity
		1 to 100 scale. An average adult has 30 or so, a child around 5-10, craftsmen and atheletes around 50, and specialized experts/gymnists around 70. Beyond 70 are considered particularly gifted, with only above 85 being considered superhuman
			Determine this number with a 4d20+10 roll
			
	Intelligence
		1 to 100 scale. An average adult has 30 or so, a child around 5-10, scholars and nobles around 50, and most accomplished wizards around 70. Upwards of 80 are considered to be unrivaled geniuses and are almost always Maker wizards if they have the gift and brilliant scientists if not
			Determine this number with a 4d20+10 roll. If your Caster Tier is above 5, instead determine this number with a ((1d10 * (Caster Tier - 1)) + 1d10) roll. 
			
	Vitality
		1 to 100 scale. An average adult has 30 or so, a child around 5-10, soliders and atheletes around 50, and experienced gladiators around 70. Beyond 70 is considered "blessed", and such people rarely get sick and live very long lives.
			Determine this number with a 4d20+10 roll.
			
	
	Your starting Base Stats impact your character's body, and should be considered when determining their Lifestyle. 
	
	Your character's height range is determined by your Strength stat, as larger people tend to be stronger. Use the following formulas to determine how tall your character can be. If your character doesn't fit into either category, feel free to use whichever feels right. Pick any value within this range for your character.
		For Men: 48 + (28 * (Strength/100)) through 56 + (28 * (Strength/100))
		For Women: 42 + (28 * (Strength/100)) through 50 + (28 * (Strength/100))
	
	As your character grew up, they came into these stats as their reached their age. People tend to do what they are good at, and thus your character lived a certain Lifestyle from childhood onward. Certain stat distributions may limit or force a certain Lifestyle, for lore reasons. 
	
	Your lifestyle determines 3 things: your past, your starting abilities, and your starting items. You have a certain amount of EXP you can put into your skills to indicate your experience with them.
